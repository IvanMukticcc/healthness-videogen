ADDING A TOPIC TO base_<topic>.png   —   VITAMINS
=================================================

The base layer already contains everything that must never change: the five row
background stripes, the five liquid waves, the title and the Healthness logo.
The waves are the animated part, and the animation reads them from
ribbon_mask.png, which was authored from this exact geometry. If a wave moves by
even a few pixels, the mask no longer fits and the video breaks.

**THIS VARIANT ASKS FOR HALF OF WHAT ORGANS ASKS FOR.** The generator adds a bowl
on the left of every row and *nothing at all* on the right. The right guide
circle is where the vitamin ball goes, and the ball is composited afterwards by
`vitamin_overlay.py` out of `engine/micro/icons/` - the app's own ball, at the
app's own blue, with the letter already on it.

That is deliberate and it is the same trade the rest of this repository makes:
what must be identical is never regenerated. A generator asked for "the vitamin C
in this food" draws pills one time, a bottle the next and an orange the third,
and it cannot draw the letter at all, because the prompt forbids text in three
places and has to. So the one element on the poster that has to be exactly right
is the one element not left to a roll of the dice.

No text of any kind - the title is drawn into the base and the row captions go on
afterwards with add_labels.py.

Attach the base for the topic (base_<topic>.png, not base_layer.png) and use the
prompt below.

  logo                           y ~2494-2576, centred

  guide circles   r 137, centred x 250 and x 1270, one per row over each wave tip
  caption bars    380 x 78, centred under each circle, 20px below it

  Both tips of the wave sit on a circle centre, within a pixel, so a bowl that
  fills its circle takes the liquid in at its middle.

  The bowl fills the LEFT circle. The RIGHT circle is left exactly as it arrives.
  Both caption bars stay empty: add_labels.py writes the food into the left one
  and the vitamin into the right one.

  The safe area in the prompt is one FOURTEENTH of the width, not one tenth.
  The circles reach x 112 and x 1408, and one tenth is 154 - so a tenth tells the
  model to keep the bowl out of the very circle it is told to fill, and it obeys
  the sentence rather than the mark. A fourteenth is 110, just outside them.

WHY THE PROMPT LOOKS REPETITIVE

Every line of it was paid for once, most of them in the Organs template this one
was split from on 16 September 2026.

- The camera angle is stated twice: once for all five bowls, then again inside
  every row. Said only at the top it holds for the first row or two and the rest
  come out flat, seen straight down - a disc of food, not a bowl. Said only in
  the rows it is ignored as decoration. Both, and all five are in perspective.
- "its rim an ellipse and the side of the bowl clearly visible, the food heaped
  above the rim" is the same instruction made checkable. A three-quarter angle is
  a phrase the model can nod at; an elliptical rim and a visible side wall are
  things it either draws or does not.
- The bowl's material is named in every row too. "A bowl of walnuts" lets the
  model pick: one poster came back with clear glass, another with opaque ceramic
  in five different colours, off the same wording. Glass is the one that sits
  next to a liquid without competing with it, so it is stated, not left to the
  roll.
- The no-text rule is split in two - the title, then everything else. Combined in
  one paragraph the model reads it as being about the heading only and still puts
  a word under each bowl.
- The bars are described in the same breath as the circles, because they are the
  same kind of mark: one to fill, one to leave alone. Mentioned in a paragraph of
  their own they get treated as part of the background and covered.
- "No part of the circle is still visible" is not enough on its own, and cost a
  poster to learn. A generator satisfied it by painting a bright disc over the
  circle and standing the bowl on that - the mark was gone, and a plate had taken
  its place, 140 to 155 levels off the base, which no threshold catches without
  eating the bowl. So the circles are named as alignment marks that are not
  artwork, and the sentence after it forbids the replacement by name. Saying what
  must disappear is only half of it; the other half is saying that nothing may
  take its place.


AND THE TWO PARAGRAPHS THAT ARE THIS VARIANT'S OWN

The empty right circle is the new thing here, and an empty half of a row is the
most suggestive thing you can hand a generator. Both paragraphs exist because
"leave it empty" is not one instruction, it is two, and they fail differently:

- **The circle itself.** "Leave it exactly as it appears" rather than "leave it
  blank" or "remove the mark", because untouched is the state the animator can
  detect: where the poster still equals the anchored base, `flowanim.py` puts the
  clean base back and the mark is gone for free. A generator that helpfully tidies
  the ring away has done no harm either, but a generator that paints *something*
  there has, and the ball then lands on top of it. The list of nouns is long on
  purpose - capsule, pill, tablet, bottle, fruit, leaf, garnish - because the ones
  a model reaches for when told "vitamin" are exactly the ones it will reach for
  when told nothing and left a circle.
- **The space.** Forbidding the circle does not forbid the row. A model that has
  been told five times that a bowl goes on the left and nothing goes on the right
  will still widen the bowl, or lay the food out across the stripe towards the
  gap, because a composition with one heavy side looks unfinished to it. So the
  emptiness is named as correct and intended, in its own paragraph, and the ways
  of filling it that are not "putting something in the circle" are listed.

There is also a rule this variant does not need and Organs cannot avoid, and it is
worth knowing why: ../../CLAUDE.md rule 10, that a body part named by its address
draws everything at that address. Nothing here is named by an address, because
nothing here is a body part. That is one whole class of regeneration this template
cannot suffer.


WHY THERE IS NO NEGATIVE PROMPT

There was one in the parent template until 8 September and it was removed on the
user's report: the generator sometimes deletes what the base drew - the reserved
caption bars among them - and the separate negative field is where that comes
from. The list said `text, letters, caption, label, title` and `changed logo`,
while the body of the same prompt says the title is already in the image, the logo
is final and the bars are reserved and must be left completely clear. Handed both,
a generator can read the negative field as permission to remove exactly those
things.

The body carries all of it already, and carries it in context. Do not add one
back. If a generation comes back wrong, the fix is a sentence in the body that
names the thing and its place - which is what every entry in flow.md's "what is
already known to break" turned out to be.


AFTER GENERATING

Check the wave before animating, from work/ - the tools are the engine's and
are called by path, never copied in (../../CLAUDE.md rule 1):

    ../.venv/bin/python ../../engine/check_base.py <topic>_poster.jpeg \
        --base ../INPUT/base_<topic>.png

It reports how far each wave has drifted from the base layer. Under about two
pixels the authored mask still fits. Above that, regenerate rather than animate:
the animation cannot correct a wave the generator has redrawn.

Then look at the right-hand circles with your own eyes. `check_base.py` measures
the wave, not the emptiness, and a bowl that crept rightwards or a stray leaf on
the stripe is something only a person sees. The ball is 290px across and centred
on the mark, so it hides anything inside the circle and nothing at all outside it.
