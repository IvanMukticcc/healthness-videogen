TOPICS THAT HAVE SHIPPED, AND HOW TO REBUILD THEM
=================================================

`base_<topic>.png`, its clean copy and its layout are all gitignored - they come
out of one `recolor_base.py` call and a tool can make them again in seconds. That
is true only if the call is written down. It is written down here.

Micro and Exercise both keep a file like this and this folder went four topics
without one, which meant four palettes existed in exactly one place: a PNG that
git does not carry.

Each entry is everything needed to get back to a finished clip: the base, the
captions, the hacks with their clocks, and which mode it shipped in.

    GLYPH MODE   `./render.sh <topic> 'auto:...'` - no generator, about a minute
    SCENE MODE   the poster round trip. `ImageSwap.txt` has the prompt; the
                 filled scenes are kept with the topic below


PALETTE RULE FOR THIS VARIANT
-----------------------------

**The wave takes the colour of the hour.** Foods gives its wave the colour of its
food; a hack is not a colour, so this replaces it - dawn amber, morning ice,
midday lime, afternoon coffee, night violet, read top to bottom.

Two constraints on top of it, and the second only appeared with scene mode:

- rows 1, 3, 5 are the dark stripe and 2, 4 the light one, so the bright wave
  colours go on the dark rows. The glyph is drawn in its own wave's colour and a
  pale wave on a pale row is a pale glyph on a pale disc
- **in scene mode every wave should be something that exists in its own
  photograph.** The join only works if the subject can match the wave's colour
  where they meet, and a colour chosen for the arc alone leaves it nothing to
  match. `sleep` is the first palette picked that way throughout: dawn gold,
  coffee brown, the warm red of a lamp over dinner, screen indigo, night violet

The palette is otherwise unrestricted, which is this variant's alone - the chips
are glass and the dials have their own discs, so nothing on the liquid has a hue
to collide with. Exercise has to keep its waves off red, amber and orange.

  --rows '#0E1A20,#E8EEF0' \
  --waves '#FFB020,#1B8FD6,#9BE04A,#B4471E,#A98CFF' \
  --vibrance 1.05 --contrast 1.04 \
  --title '5 FREE BIOHACKS' --subtitle 'ONE DAY, ZERO COST' \
  -o ../INPUT/base_day.png --work .

../../.venv/bin/python ../../engine/add_labels.py ../INPUT/base_day.png \
  -l base_day_layout.json -o day_labelled.png \
  --labels 'MORNING LIGHT|BEDTIME,COLD FINISH|DOPAMINE,LUNCH WALK|GLUCOSE PEAK,LAST COFFEE|CAFFEINE LEFT,LIGHTS DOWN|MELATONIN'

./render.sh day 'auto:MORNING LIGHT,COLD FINISH,WALK AFTER LUNCH,LAST COFFEE,LIGHTS DOWN' day_labelled.png

The first one. Clocks come from the table unchanged, which is why they run the
whole day. The palette needed no rebuild for scene mode - all five already had a
source in a picture: dawn, water, sunlit leaves, coffee, a violet glow.

**Every one of its five hacks already had a scene in another topic** - three in
`sleep`, two in `firsthour`, one in `sugar` - so written naively this would have
been a visual remix of two clips already shot. The framing was made deliberately
different instead, and that is the whole of the work on this topic:

  1 dark   a person in an open doorway, silhouetted, facing a low amber sun that
           floods past their shoulders     (not a bedroom window, not a curtain)
  2 light  two cupped hands under a running tap in a bright white bathroom
           (not a shoulder in a dark shower - and on the LIGHT band, where
           firsthour's cold row was dark)
  3 dark   a shaded path under trees, low sun through the leaves so they glow
           lime against the dark          (not sugar's open sunlit park)
  4 light  a hand pushing a half-full cup away across a bright table
           (not a pour - and pushing it away is what "last" coffee looks like)
  5 dark   a hand turning a wall dimmer down, the room dropping to violet
           (not a bedside lamp)

Check the overlap before writing scenes for any new topic. Two topics can share
a hack without any harm; they cannot share its photograph.

as one argument rather than as five facts. Worth doing again on a topic that has
one direction.

photograph: teal for standing at a desk, magenta for walking, violet for dinner.
Every wave now comes out of something in its own scene - roasted peppers, a
green park path, cool window daylight, golden-hour pavement, a dusk sky behind a
table - which is the rule this file states above and the first topic to have
been rebuilt to satisfy it.

The left caption is `WALK MORE`, not `7000 STEPS`, because the chip already says
7000 STEPS and the row would spend its whole half second saying one thing twice.

The first topic that needed the table grown rather than rearranged - `fibre
first`, `lift twice` and `see people` are new, each with its own meta-analysis.
Four topics in, every hack was being used twice and a fifth would have been a
remix.

**Five studies of one outcome want five different words for it.** All-cause
mortality is what four of these actually measured, and five rows captioned
`DEATH RISK` is one row shown five times. Each caption is the outcome that
study reported - all causes, death risk, diabetes, cardiac risk, survival - so
they are true and they are distinct. Do not reach for a synonym that the source
does not support to break a tie; change the entry instead.

The clocks are the weakest part and worth knowing about. A longevity topic is
not a day, and 08:00 to 20:30 is a plausible day rather than a measured one -
the studies say nothing about when. The day bar still earns its place because
these are things you do in a day, but if a topic ever has no honest clocks,
`--daybar 0` is there.

`SEE PEOPLE` is the only entry in the table whose chip is not a number, because
the studies measure social integration rather than minutes. It is also the
largest effect on the poster, which is worth leaving where the eye finds it.

**The left caption was fixed with words, not with a generation.** `STEP OUTSIDE`
put its S and T on the tree trunk: 25.9% of the strip within 60 of the ink,
19.8% after the plate, against a limit of 20 - passing on two tenths of a point,
and the S visibly going when cropped and looked at. The caption is centred on
x=250, so a shorter string starts further right: `GO OUTSIDE` measures 20.1%
before the plate and **12.0%** after, and the letters clear the trunk entirely.

That is worth knowing as a move. The plate is the answer when the background
under a caption is busy everywhere; when it is one dark mass at one end, two
characters off the caption is free and a generation is not. It only works
because `add_labels.py` centres - a left-aligned caption would not have moved.

Two new entries, and `sleep enough` is the best-designed study in the table:

**Three topics were considered and rejected, and the reasons are the useful
part.** A "biggest numbers" ranking would put +250% dopamine beside -47%
mortality in one ordered column, and this table's whole ethic is that each

support. Gargling (Satomura 2005, -36% URTI) is a real RCT and a lovely free

to Prather and Holt-Lunstad, that is the row a viewer chases and finds nothing
behind. And Araujo's ten-second one-leg stand (BJSM 2022, +84% mortality if you
fail it) measures a TEST predicting death, not practising balance preventing it.
Using it would have been bending a study to fit a hack.

================================================================================
TITLES ARE THE ONE THING THAT MAY NOT REPEAT
================================================================================

`check_scene.py` and `engine/check_base.py` both catch a poster generated from
another topic's base by comparing the title band, and both go quiet when two
bases share a title - Micro's superfoods run measures 5.4 across its own topics
and passes. Every title above is unique across all 49 bases in the repository.

This is the variant most likely to break that, because it is built to run series
fast and a series wants one title. If a series is wanted, number it into the
title - `SLEEP FIXES 1`, `SLEEP FIXES 2` - rather than leaving it to the topic
name, which the check never sees.
