ADDING A TOPIC TO base_<topic>.png
==================================

The base layer already contains everything that must never change: the five row
background stripes, the five liquid waves, the title and the Healthness logo.
The waves are the animated part, and the animation reads them from
ribbon_mask.png, which was authored from this exact geometry. If a wave moves by
even a few pixels, the mask no longer fits and the video breaks.

In the food version the generator drew both ends of every row: a bowl on the
left and an organ on the right. Here it draws ONLY THE LEFT: the lift being
performed. The right circle holds the body with its working muscles lit, and
that is drawn by bodymap.py rather than asked for.

That is the one structural difference between the two versions, and it is not a
convenience. Five rows whose bodies disagree about where the lats are is five
different bodies, and the comparison the whole clip is about - this lift hits
that, the next one hits this - does not survive it. An image model cannot draw
the same anatomy twice, so it is not asked to, for the same reason it is not
asked to redraw the wave.

So the generator's job is only to ADD the athlete on the left of every row. No
text of any kind - the title is drawn into the base and the row captions go on
afterwards with add_labels.py.

Attach the base for the topic (base_<topic>.png, not base_layer.png) and use the
prompt below.

  logo                           y ~2494-2576, centred

  guide circles   r 137, centred x 250 and x 1270, one per row over each wave tip
  caption bars    380 x 78, centred under each circle, 20px below it

  Both tips of the wave sit on a circle centre, within a pixel, so a figure that
  fills the left circle takes the liquid out of the lift at its middle.

  LEFT circle   a filled disc in the colour of that row's own wave, drawn into
                the base by left_disc.py. The athlete stands in it, and the
                liquid then leaves a circle of its own colour - which is what
                the bowl does in the food version. The generator only adds the
                athlete; the circle is not its to draw, recolour or replace.
  RIGHT circle  the body map. Drawn by bodymap.py over a disc that covers the
                circle exactly. LEAVE IT ALONE.
  caption bars  add_labels.py writes into them. Leave them alone.

  The safe area in the prompt is one FOURTEENTH of the width, not one tenth.
  The circles reach x 112 and x 1408, and one tenth is 154 - so a tenth tells the
  model to keep the athlete out of the very circle it is told to fill, and it
  obeys the sentence rather than the mark. A fourteenth is 110, just outside them.

WHY THE PROMPT LOOKS REPETITIVE

Most of it was paid for once, by the food version, and against the same
generator behaviours.

- The camera angle is stated twice: once for all five athletes, then again
  inside every row. Said only at the top it holds for the first row or two and
  the rest drift. Said only in the rows it is ignored as decoration.
- "caught at the hardest point of the rep, the working muscle under visible
  tension" is the same instruction made checkable, the way "its rim an ellipse
  and the side of the bowl clearly visible" was for the bowls. A dynamic pose is
  a phrase the model can nod at; the bottom of a squat is a thing it either
  draws or does not.
- The no-text rule is split in two - the title, then everything else. Combined in
  one paragraph the model reads it as being about the heading only and still puts
  a word under each figure.
- The bars are described in the same breath as the circles, because they are the
  same kind of mark: one to fill, one to leave alone.
- The right circle is called FINISHED ARTWORK rather than "empty". The circles
  work as instructions because the model fills what it can see, which is exactly
  why an empty one on the right is a standing invitation. Naming it as already
  done, and then listing what not to put there, is the strongest form available
  without redrawing the base.

  There is a safety net underneath it, and it was measured rather than assumed.
  flowanim.py restores the right circle to the clean base wherever the poster
  still matches, then paints the body's disc over the full circle radius at 0.97
  alpha. Rendering a poster with a white blob filling every right circle, and
  diffing it against the same poster without them:

      inside the circle      3-13 levels of residual, 25 px above 15 in all
      at the circle's edge   up to 40 levels, on about 180 px

  So a stray organ INSIDE the circle is gone - three per cent of it survives, and
  three per cent of white on a near-black row is a couple of levels. Something
  straddling the EDGE is not: it survives where it overhangs, and it also shifts
  the wave's tip a little, because the ribbon geometry and the pinned-artwork
  pass are both computed from the poster. That case is a regeneration.


NEGATIVE PROMPT, if the tool offers a separate field:

AFTER GENERATING

Check the wave before animating:

    ../.venv/bin/python ../../engine/check_base.py new_poster.png --base base_<topic>.png

It reports how far each wave has drifted from the base layer. Under about two
pixels the authored mask still fits. Above that, regenerate rather than animate:
the animation cannot correct a wave the generator has redrawn.

Then look at the right circles by eye. The wave check does not cover them, and
what the safety net can and cannot absorb is the paragraph above.
