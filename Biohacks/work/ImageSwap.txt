ADDING A TOPIC TO base_<topic>.png
==================================

There are two ways to make a Biohacks poster and they want different prompts.

  GLYPH MODE   (the default, no generator at all)
      Both circles are drawn at render time - a lucide glyph on the left, a
      counting dial on the right - and `./render.sh <topic> 'auto:...'` is the
      whole loop. Nothing in this file applies. See flow.md.

  SCENE MODE   (this file)
      Every row becomes a photograph, edge to edge, running behind the liquid,
      with the hack happening in it on the left. Our wave, our chips, our dials,
      our captions and our day bar go on top afterwards. It costs the round trip
      and it buys a poster that looks like a place rather than a diagram.

          ./render.sh <topic> 'auto:...' <topic>_labelled.png --scene poster

      `--scene poster` drops the glyph and its disc, keeps the pulse, and puts
      the dials on glass instead of on a row colour - there is no row colour to
      take when the row is a photograph.


WHAT THE GENERATOR MAY AND MAY NOT TOUCH

    the five waves     NEVER. This is the whole design. They are authored once
                       and animated from a mask that fits them to the pixel; a
                       wave moved by two pixels breaks the video
    the row stripes    replaced by photographs - that is the point of this mode
    the left circle    covered by the scene. The subject stands where it was,
                       but it is not cut into a circle any more
    the right circle   the background may run through it. Nothing may be DRAWN
                       in it: the dial covers it at render time
    the caption bars   kept simple. The text goes on afterwards in a fixed
                       colour and needs somewhere quiet to land
    the strip below    the day bar's, and the logo's. Untouched
    the title          already in the image


THE ONE THING THAT DECIDES WHETHER THIS WORKS

**Each row must keep its lightness.** Rows 1, 3 and 5 are dark and rows 2 and 4
are light, and `add_labels.py` picks white or near-black ink from that flag in
the layout file, before it has ever seen the photograph. A dark scene on row 2
does not make the caption grey - it makes it invisible. Say it in the prompt,
say it again per row, and check it by eye before labelling.


GEOMETRY OF THE BASE LAYER  (1536 x 2752)

  waves span x 229-1289          = 15% to 84% of the width
  row 1 wave  y  491- 702        row stripes start at 412, 825, 1238, 1649, 2063
  row 2 wave  y  904-1115        each stripe 413 tall
  row 3 wave  y 1317-1527
  row 4 wave  y 1729-1940
  row 5 wave  y 2142-2353
  title                          y ~191-392
  caption bars    380 x 78, centred under each circle
  day bar         y 2476-2585, drawn at render time
  logo            y ~2586-2667

  guide circles   r 137, centred x 250 and x 1270

  THE SAFE AREA APPLIES TO THE SUBJECT, NOT TO THE BACKGROUND. The photograph
  should reach all four edges of its row; what must stay a fourteenth of the
  width clear of the left and right edges is the thing the row is about. In
  glyph mode the safe area covered everything, and a prompt copied from there
  produces five photographs with a margin of row colour around them.


=== THE PROMPT ===  (fill the five rows, paste with base_<topic>.png attached)

Use the attached image as the base. Keep everything I list as final and replace only what I ask for.

THE FIVE COLOURED LIQUID WAVES ARE FINAL AND MUST NOT CHANGE. Do not move them, do not redraw them, do not restyle them, do not change their shape, their length, their thickness, their colour or their gloss. Do not turn them into splashes, spray, droplets, mist or particles. Do not add a glow, a halo, a shadow or a reflection around them. They must come back pixel for pixel where they are now — everything else in the image is being replaced around them.

THE TITLE AT THE TOP AND THE LOGO AT THE BOTTOM ARE FINAL. Do not redraw, move or restyle either. ADD NO TEXT OF ANY KIND: no labels, no captions, no numbers, no clock times, no watermarks. All text is added later, outside the generator.

REPLACE EACH OF THE FIVE HORIZONTAL BANDS WITH A PHOTOGRAPH. Each band is a full-bleed photographic scene that fills its band from the left edge to the right edge and from its top edge to its bottom edge, running behind the liquid wave. The five bands are five separate photographs with a clean horizontal cut between them — they do not blend into each other.

KEEP EACH BAND'S LIGHTNESS EXACTLY AS IT IS NOW. Bands 1, 3 and 5 are dark and their photographs must be dark overall — night, shadow, deep tone. Bands 2 and 4 are light and their photographs must be light overall — bright, airy, pale. This is not a style preference: text is added afterwards in a fixed colour and a band that changes its lightness loses its caption.

THE SUBJECT OF EACH ROW SITS ON THE LEFT, in the left third of the band, where the faint circle is now. It is part of the photograph and bleeds into the scene — do not cut it into a circle, do not put a disc, frame, vignette or border behind it. The liquid leaves the subject at its right-hand side.

THE FAINT CIRCLE ON THE RIGHT OF EACH BAND: the photograph may run straight through it. Do not draw anything in it and do not draw anything around it — no object, no person, no ring, no dial, no gauge, no chart, no glow. It is covered by finished artwork later.

KEEP THE BOTTOM FIFTH OF EACH BAND QUIET — simple, low-detail, low-contrast, no faces and no small busy texture. Captions are placed there afterwards and need somewhere calm to sit.

LEAVE THE BAND BELOW THE LAST ROW EMPTY, between the bottom row and the logo. Nothing goes there.

The photographs are realistic, cinematic and shot on a real camera: shallow depth of field, natural light, one clear subject, no studio backdrop, no illustration, no 3D render, no stock-photo staging.

Row 1 (dark): <SCENE 1>

Row 2 (light): <SCENE 2>

Row 3 (dark): <SCENE 3>

Row 4 (light): <SCENE 4>

Row 5 (dark): <SCENE 5>

=== END OF PROMPT ===


WRITING THE FIVE SCENES

Name the moment, not the category. A hack asked for as a noun comes back as a
product shot: "cold exposure" gives a shower head on white, "a hand held under
cold running water, droplets caught mid-air" gives the thing itself. This is the
same lesson the food version writes down as "an organ named on its own comes back
as an object" and the exercise version as "caught at the hardest point of the
rep" — a phrase the model can nod at is worth less than a thing it either draws
or does not.

And say the tone with the scene, every time. Stated once at the top it holds for
a row or two and then drifts, which is what happened to the camera angle in the
food version and is written down there.


NEGATIVE PROMPT, if the tool offers a separate field:

text, letters, numbers, caption, label, title, clock, watermark, logo, ring,
dial, gauge, chart, infographic, diagram, object in the right circle, circle
around the subject, disc behind the subject, frame, border, vignette, collage,
split screen, illustration, 3d render, cartoon, changed liquid, redrawn wave,
restyled wave, wave as splash, wave as spray, droplets, mist, particles,
sparkles, glow around the liquid, halo, drop shadow, reflection, moved wave,
recoloured wave, changed title, changed logo, anything below the bottom row


AFTER GENERATING

    ../../.venv/bin/python ../../engine/grab.py <topic>
    ../../.venv/bin/python ../../engine/check_base.py <topic>.jpeg \
        --base ../INPUT/base_<topic>.png

Under about two pixels of drift the authored mask still fits. Above that,
regenerate — the animation cannot correct a wave the generator has redrawn, and
in this mode there is more to tempt it: a photograph of water next to a liquid
wave is an invitation to merge the two.

Then, by eye, before labelling:

  - is every band still the lightness it started as? Rows 1, 3, 5 dark, 2 and 4
    light. This is the one that silently ruins the captions
  - is the strip under the bottom row still empty? The day bar goes there and
    has no disc to hide behind
  - is anything DRAWN in a right circle? The dial covers what is inside it, but
    something straddling its edge survives and also nudges the wave's tip

WHY THE LIQUID STILL ANIMATES OVER A PHOTOGRAPH

Worth knowing, because it looks like it should not. The animator finds artwork by
difference from `base_<topic>_clean.png` and paints only what is left. A
full-bleed photograph differs from the flat base everywhere, so the whole row
becomes artwork — except the wave, which the prompt above forbids touching and
which therefore still matches the base exactly. The wave is the only clean thing
left, and the clean thing is what gets painted.

The engine already defends the one way this could go wrong: a wave enclosed on
all sides by artwork is an enclosed hole, and `binary_fill_holes` would fill it
and freeze the liquid silently. `--art-cap` stops that — a hole bigger than 25%
of the row's wave is not filled, and this one is the whole of it.
